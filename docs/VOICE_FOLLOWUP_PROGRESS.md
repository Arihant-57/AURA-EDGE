## Timer announcements

The source was the automated voice replay on port 8034, using the disposable `aura_voice_verify` database. Between 06:48:30 and 06:52:00 IST, twelve different test timers produced twelve notifications. Ten were marked spoken: six English, three Hindi and one Kannada. Nine timers expired normally and three active, overdue timers were marked missed once at the audit backend's restart. The normal backend had no matching timer completion records and its 40 timer records were cancelled. Voice metrics in the incident window showed 60 uploaded recordings and 11 typed requests in QA, with zero microphone or continuous-listening turns. The normal backend recorded zero turns. [Timer IDs, notification IDs, times and delivery flags](../qa/results/audit-timer-incident.json)

The audit launcher now requires an explicit alternate-port loopback PostgreSQL database and defaults to silent synthesis. Only the interactive microphone mode opens the speaker for direct replies. It keeps proactive alerts silent. The normal application keeps its existing spoken notification settings, half-duplex capture and scheduler. The audit processes that caused the announcements were stopped. The five-minute run stayed idle for 301.3 seconds over 31 observations, with zero new timers and notifications. [Idle results](../qa/results/audit-timer-idle.json) Eight regression tests passed, including simulated English and Hindi delivery, repeated polling, duplicate notification calls, restart, the database guard and suppression at the speaker boundary.

## Multilingual voice

On 30 identical newly synthesized recordings, the checkpoint scored 19/30 and the updated parser scored 20/30. English held at 9/10 and Hindi at 7/8. Kannada improved from 2/7 to 3/7, Hindi-English remained 1/3 and Kannada-English remained 0/2. This matched subset omits the three saved recordings and two non-speech checks from the earlier shipping figures. Kannada accepts a short-vowel pronunciation of “on” only with an explicit room, appliance and positive imperative. It also restores damaged timer word boundaries while preserving the duration and verb. The other failed transcripts still omit or corrupt the room, timer verb, number or power direction. The subsequent consolidated run with fresh synthesis passed 66/66 typed cases (53/53 specifying an intent) and 24/35 recordings. English passed 11/11, Hindi 7/9 and Kannada 3/8. The mixed-language counts held at 1/3 and 0/2, and both non-speech controls passed. No failed recording reported an action in either run. Fresh Hindi failures included a corrupted seconds unit and a damaged AC request. The earlier 9/9 Hindi result did not reproduce. ## Conversational replies

The existing client already sends `think: false`, uses a 4,096-token context and asks Ollama to keep the model loaded for 15 minutes. The first checkpoint run spent 5.04 seconds reaching the first model token. Its three warm repeats took 0.27–0.29 seconds for the same stage and had median first-speaker output of 1.82 seconds. That variation is consistent with model loading and host load affecting the earlier 11.04-second result. Complete-answer validation still precedes synthesis. The revised prompt shortened the warm median from 37 to 25 words and passed all eight factual checks. Median warm time through synthesis measured from routing completion was 1.75 seconds at the checkpoint and 1.91 seconds after the prompt change. A first-speaker latency improvement has yet to be demonstrated.
